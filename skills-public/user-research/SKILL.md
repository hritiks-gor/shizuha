# User Research

Start with a research question and the decision it will inform. Select participants relevant to that question and document sampling limitations. Choose an interview, task-based usability session, survey or observation method that can answer it.

Explain participation, recording, retention and quote usage clearly, and obtain the required consent. Collect only necessary information. Store identifiable evidence with appropriate access controls; use anonymized synthesis for wider sharing.

Ask open questions about concrete past behavior. In usability sessions, give realistic tasks without revealing the intended path. Observe completion, errors and workarounds rather than teaching the interface or pitching a preferred solution.

Separate direct observations and quotations from interpretations. Summarize patterns with context, severity and confidence, including contradictory evidence. Recommend the next decision or experiment and explain how the evidence supports it. Do not present a handful of interviews as a statistically representative result.
